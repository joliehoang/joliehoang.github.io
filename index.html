About

I'm a PhD candidate in the Beckmann and Charney labs (Brain and Data Sciences) at Mount Sinai. My work combines genomics, transcriptomics, neuroimaging, and physiological data to understand the living human brain and psychiatric illness, with a focus on methods that hold up to scrutiny.

Before my PhD I spent six years in psychosis neuroimaging research at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center / Harvard Medical School, earned an MA in Psychology in Education (Clinical Psychology Program) at Teachers College, Columbia University, and co-managed a lab studying stigma and psychosis at Columbia and NYSPI. I speak Vietnamese, English, and Mandarin, and I've spent much of my time outside the lab building programs that bring mental health and science to more people.

Research

Reading the living brain from blood

With the Living Brain Project, I build machine learning models (e.g., elastic net) that use blood transcriptomics and neuroimaging to predict gene and protein expression in living human brain tissue. We find that the strongest reproducible blood–brain concordance signals are not genetically driven.

First-author manuscript in preparation

Genomic foundation models

I contribute to DNT, a diploid-aware genomic foundation model developed in partnership with NVIDIA, and related work on JEPA-DNA, GFMBench-API, and VANDAM. I also benchmark deep learning sequence-to-expression models (Enformer, Basenji2, ExPecto, Xpresso) on personal genomes against a simple population-average baseline across ~1,470 brain and blood samples.

JEPA-DNA · DNT · GFMBench-API · VANDAM

Rigor in gene expression analysis

Using large-scale simulations and 14 brain cohorts across five neuropsychiatric disorders, I showed that indiscriminate surrogate variable adjustment leads to overfitting in differential expression analyses.

Preprint · Code · ASHG & ESHG 2024

Self-supervised models of physiology

I contribute to joint-embedding (JEPA) models that learn from physiological signals for real-time ICU risk estimation (PhysioJEPA) and from at-home sleep data for disease-risk estimation (SleepJEPA).

PhysioJEPA, ML4H 2025 · NeurIPS 2025 workshops

AI for mental health

I contribute design input on infrastructure and UI/UX for Psybot, an AI agent designed to reduce the conviction of delusions in people with psychotic disorders (ongoing study, 11 patients enrolled), and am co-first author of a study showing that an independent supervisory safety agent improves detection of suicidal ideation in large language models.

Under review at npj Digital Medicine

Psychosis neuroimaging

Six years of multimodal MRI research on psychosis, covering brain morphology, cerebral perfusion, connectomics, and their links to inflammation, cognition, and symptoms in multisite cohorts.

Psychological Medicine 2026 · Brain, Behavior, and Immunity 2022
