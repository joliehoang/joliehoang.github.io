About

I grew up in Hà Nội, Việt Nam, in a family of healers, and moved to the U.S. at fourteen. Seeing how unevenly care reaches people gave me a lasting purpose: helping people overcome disease and regain control over their lives, especially those born into harder circumstances.

I learned science from generous mentors. During six years at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center and Harvard Medical School, Dr. Paulo Lizano and Dr. Matcheri Keshavan taught me to think critically, work rigorously, and produce knowledge rather than only consume it. During my master’s at Teachers College, Columbia University, Dr. Lawrence Yang and Dr. Ragy Girgis brought me into global mental health research in understudied communities.

Today I’m a PhD candidate in the labs of Dr. Alexander Charney and Dr. Noam Beckmann at Mount Sinai. I build machine learning on blood–brain multi-omics and neuroimaging from the Living Brain Project, and help develop genomic foundation models with NVIDIA and Sheba Medical Center. A thread through my work is making sure results hold up: my studies of correction methods in gene expression analysis and of benchmarks for genomic deep learning give researchers practical checks for building on solid ground. I bring the same care to AI for mental health, including an independent safety agent that helps AI systems notice when someone may be in crisis.

Outside research, I teach statistics, have led a mental health video series in three languages, and love building communities around science. I speak Vietnamese, English, and Mandarin.

Research

Predicting brain gene expression from blood and DNA

With the Living Brain Project, I build machine learning models that predict gene and protein expression in living human brain tissue from blood transcriptomics, genetics, and neuroimaging. We find that the strongest reproducible blood–brain concordance signals are not genetically driven. I also benchmark deep learning models that predict brain and blood gene expression from a person’s genome (Enformer, Basenji2, ExPecto, Xpresso) against a simple population-average baseline across ~1,470 samples.

First-author manuscripts in preparation.

Genomic foundation models

I contribute to DNT, a diploid-aware genomic foundation model developed in partnership with NVIDIA and Sheba Medical Center, and related work on JEPA-DNA, GFMBench-API, and VANDAM. Instead of reading a single reference sequence, these models learn from both copies of a person’s genome.

JEPA-DNA · DNT · GFMBench-API · VANDAM

Rigor in gene expression analysis

Using large-scale simulations and 14 brain cohorts across five neuropsychiatric disorders, I showed that indiscriminate surrogate variable adjustment leads to overfitting in differential expression analyses.

Preprint · Code · ASHG & ESHG 2024

Self-supervised models of physiology

I contribute to joint-embedding (JEPA) models that learn from physiological signals for real-time ICU risk estimation (PhysioJEPA) and from at-home sleep data for disease-risk estimation (SleepJEPA).

PhysioJEPA, ML4H 2025 · NeurIPS 2025 workshops

AI chatbot for mental health

I contribute design input on infrastructure and UI/UX for Psybot, an AI agent designed to reduce the conviction of delusions in people with psychotic disorders, and am co-first author of a study showing that an independent supervisory safety agent improves detection of suicidal ideation in large language models.

First-author manuscript under review.

Linking psychosis neuroimaging and symptom severity

Six years of multimodal MRI research on psychosis, covering brain morphology, cerebral perfusion, connectomics, and their links to inflammation, cognition, and symptoms in multisite cohorts.

Psychological Medicine 2026 · Brain, Behavior, and Immunity 2022 · Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging 2021
