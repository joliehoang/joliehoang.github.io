About

I grew up in Hà Nội, Việt Nam, in a family of healers, and moved to the U.S. on my own at fourteen. Seeing how slowly and unevenly care reaches people shaped what I want to do: use technology to improve lives at scale, especially for those born into harder circumstances.

Before turning to AI, I spent six years at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center and Harvard Medical School with Dr. Paulo Lizano and Dr. Matcheri Keshavan, using neuroimaging (structural MRI and arterial spin labeling) to look for brain markers linked to symptom severity in psychotic disorders. During my master’s at Teachers College, Columbia University, mentored by Dr. Lawrence Yang, Dr. Ragy Girgis, and Dr. Randall Richardson-Vejlgaard, I continued studying psychosis and expanded into global mental health research in understudied communities.

Today I’m a PhD candidate in the labs of Dr. Alexander Charney and Dr. Noam Beckmann at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. My research sits at the intersection of neuroscience, genetics, and AI: I build machine learning models on blood, brain, and genetic data from the Living Brain Project, and help develop genomic foundation models with NVIDIA and Sheba Medical Center. Because results depend on methods, I’ve studied when correction methods in gene expression analysis help or hurt, and how to benchmark genomic deep learning models fairly. I’m also part of a team building and testing a therapeutic AI chatbot for people with psychotic disorders.

Outside research, I love to travel, stay active, and play card games. These days I’m also learning wakesurfing, martial arts, and 3D printing.

Research

Predicting brain gene expression from blood and DNA

With the Living Brain Project, I study gene and protein expression in living human brain tissue through three projects:

  • Blood to brain: machine learning that predicts brain expression from blood transcriptomics and neuroimaging. The strongest reproducible blood–brain concordance signals turn out not to be genetically driven.
  • Genetics to brain: how genetic variants regulate gene and protein expression in the living brain.
  • Genome to expression, fairly benchmarked: testing deep learning models (Enformer, Basenji2, ExPecto, Xpresso) against a simple population-average baseline across ~1,470 samples.

First-author manuscripts: one under review, two in preparation.

Genomic foundation models

With NVIDIA and Sheba Medical Center, I’m part of a team bringing genomic foundation models closer to real biology. Most models read DNA as a single string of letters, so we build models that read both copies of a person’s genome (DNT), learn what DNA segments do rather than only filling in missing letters (JEPA-DNA), and draw on DNA’s physical and chemical properties (VANDAM), along with a standardized way to benchmark them fairly (GFMBench-API).

JEPA-DNA · DNT · GFMBench-API · VANDAM

Rigor in gene expression analysis

Using large-scale simulations and 14 brain cohorts across five neuropsychiatric disorders, I showed that indiscriminate surrogate variable adjustment leads to overfitting in differential expression analyses.

First-author manuscript under review.

Preprint · Code

AI chatbot for mental health

I’m part of a team building an AI chatbot designed to reduce the conviction of delusions in people with psychotic disorders. My work focuses on its safety guardrails and synthetic data, including a study showing that an independent safety agent helps language models detect suicidal ideation.

First-author manuscript under review.

Global mental health and stigma

At Columbia and NYSPI, I studied how stigma and social context shape health in understudied communities: helping develop a “What Matters Most” approach to intersectional HIV and cancer stigma in Hà Nội, Việt Nam; reviewing how the COVID-19 pandemic affected chronic pain and opioid use in marginalized populations; and contributing to a multisite study of social support and help-seeking worldwide.

Intersectional stigma in Hà Nội (2024) · COVID-19, chronic pain & opioid use (2023) · Social support & help-seeking worldwide (2024)

Linking psychosis neuroimaging and symptom severity

I’ve spent six years on multimodal MRI research in psychosis, studying brain morphology, cerebral perfusion, and connectomics, and how they relate to inflammation, cognition, and symptoms in multisite cohorts.

Brain perfusion & symptoms (2026) · Inflammation & brain structure (2022) · Thalamus & hippocampus over time (2021)

Plainly

A tool I’m building to make scientific papers understandable to everyone. Good science depends on how a finding was reached, so understanding a paper should not require a PhD in that topic.
